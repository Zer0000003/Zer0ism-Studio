KNOWLEDGE BASE / 01

Philosophical atlas

Philosophy is not one tree with clean borders. Explore domains as an interconnected map: one question can belong to several branches at once.

⌕
Taxonomy is a working map, not a claim that philosophical traditions fit into exclusive boxes. Some classifications are contested.
INVESTIGATION TOOLKIT / 02

Methods, views & traditions

Compare ways of investigating philosophical questions with the traditions and positions that offer different answers. Profiles show recurring ideas, internal differences, and serious criticisms; none speaks for every thinker or settles a dispute by itself.

Approaches

23 approaches
Read profiles as maps, not labels. A tradition can contain disagreement, and a position may apply in one domain but not another. Compare the particular argument, context, and evidence rather than assigning a whole worldview to a person.
⌕

Starter profiles are selective orientation guides. They are not a complete history or substitute for reading primary works and scholarship.

REASONING WORKSPACE / 03

Argument workbench

Lay out one conclusion, the premises offered for it, an inference style, and the strongest objections and replies. Link a map to a proposition so the reasoning stays connected to the claim it addresses.

A map makes reasoning inspectable; it does not validate it. Check whether the inference fits, whether the premises are credible, and whether the strongest objection is answered. Separate subarguments can be added as their own maps.
⌕
CLAIM REGISTRY / 04

Proposition library

Store individual claims rather than accepting or rejecting whole philosophical schools. Keep source, rationale, objections, confidence, and status attached to each proposition.

Design rule: a proposition is not the same as a source, an argument, a theory, or a method. Keep these records distinct as the system grows.

RECURSIVE GOVERNANCE / 06

Audit the framework

Zer0ism must be able to examine its own evaluation rules. This audit is a structured prompt for inquiry, not an automatic proof that a position is true.

Recursive audit protocol

Mark what has been examined. Results are stored locally.

0 / 8

Failure modes to watch

Ways a meta-framework can quietly become dogmatic.

!
Immunity by definition

Defining criticism as irrational or “not sufficiently meta” so it cannot count against the system.

!
One-method absolutism

Assuming a single method can settle every empirical, conceptual, and normative question.

!
Relativism by escape hatch

Treating all views as equally justified because no view is infallible.

!
Revision theater

Claiming openness to change while never specifying what would actually change your mind.

INITIAL CHARTER / 07

Foundational charter

These are proposed procedural commitments for the first version—not sacred axioms. Each should have a rationale, limits, and a way to be challenged.

Important distinction: procedural commitments guide inquiry; substantive conclusions claim what is true or good. Neither category is automatically immune to criticism.

Conflict protocol

When principles pull in different directions.

01 · Name

State the conflicting commitments without disguising the tension.

02 · Compare

Examine context, stakes, affected parties, and the arguments for each side.

03 · Record

Document the decision, uncertainty, trade-offs, and what might justify revision.

SYSTEM DESIGN / 08

Architecture notes

Core data model

PROPOSED
01
Domain

A subject area with questions, subfields, and cross-links to other domains.

02
Tradition / theory

A historically situated position or family of positions, linked to proponents, variants, and objections.

03
Method

A procedure for investigating a question, including appropriate uses and limitations.

04
Proposition

A claim with definition, scope, source, argument, evidence, objections, status, and confidence rationale.

05
Argument

Premises and inference relations supporting or attacking a proposition; validity and soundness should not be conflated.

06
Revision event

An append-only record of what changed, who changed it, why, and what the previous version said.

07
Personal branch

A fork of selected propositions and commitments, with provenance and explicit unresolved conflicts.

Implementation status

PROTOTYPE

This is a static, browser-based prototype. Personal edits, proposition links, and argument maps are stored in localStorage on this device and browser; they are not synced or shared across devices. The taxonomy and examples are starter content, not a complete scholarly encyclopedia. Further-reading links and single-conclusion argument maps are implemented; future work can add full bibliographic records, nested argument graphs, and persistent storage.
